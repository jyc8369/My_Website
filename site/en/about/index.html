A Little About Me

A few things I enjoy, what matters to me, and how this site came about.

Things I Enjoy

Hi, I'm jyc8369.

I enjoy using AI to turn ideas into things that actually work. When I want to build something, I tend to think about how I could make it happen before asking whether it is within my current abilities. I rarely start knowing everything. I enjoy learning what I need, using the tools available to me, and gradually shaping an idea into something real.

AI is the field I'm most interested in. I don't think I need to understand it perfectly or build it from scratch before I can use it. I'm more interested in exploring what I can do with the technology available today and how new models and tools might fit into the way I work. In particular, I like using AI in the development process and connecting it to environments I manage myself.

I don't expect AI to solve every problem. It can miss the mark or produce something different from what I had in mind. I'm interested in learning how to use it well, including understanding those limitations.

I also enjoy software development. I'm more interested in the parts that make things work, such as backend systems and APIs, than in what appears on screen. I like finding out what happens behind the scenes when someone makes a request and how different components fit together. Even a simple feature can depend on many connected parts, and its structure can affect both its behavior and how it is maintained.

I'm interested in running my own services, too. Using an existing service is convenient, but when I can, I like installing and configuring software to suit my own environment. It lets me see how the pieces connect. This takes more effort, especially when setup or troubleshooting is involved, but I enjoy learning about the system along the way.

My interests also include hardware and electronics. I like building PCs, looking at components, taking devices apart, and making small repairs. I can enjoy simply learning how a device I already own is put together and what each part does. Hardware and software have something in common for me: both have structures beneath the visible result, and understanding those structures makes more experimentation possible.

I can't claim to be especially good at any one of AI, software, servers, or hardware. There is still a lot I don't know or haven't tried. What I keep enjoying is following my curiosity, finding the tools I need, and, when possible, making something work for myself.

What matters to me is less about showing how much I know and more about trying the things I'm curious about and gradually becoming able to do what I couldn't do before.

What Matters to Me

Whenever I can, I want to understand and manage the systems I use. I don't think every service has to be self-hosted just because it is convenient. A well-made hosted service can be the more sensible choice, and managing everything yourself is not always efficient.

The main reason I prefer running things myself is that I want to make as many of the decisions about the systems I use as I can.

With an external service, the provider sets the available features, terms, and pricing. That is often enough, but it can be difficult to change the details to suit your needs, and you may have to adapt when a policy changes. When I run a system myself, I can choose the software, decide which features to include, and connect its parts in a way that serves my goals.

Of course, having the freedom to choose also means taking responsibility for the result. A configuration mistake can cause problems, and every system I add creates more things to maintain. Considering the time and effort involved, self-hosting is not always the better option. Still, I value being able to shape a system the way I want and investigate a problem so I can choose how to fix it.

Running a system myself is also a way to learn how it works. I don't think I need to understand every internal detail perfectly. I simply prefer an environment where I can look into its structure when needed and change the parts I want to.

Cost is another important factor. With services billed by usage, it can be hard to predict what the final bill will look like. This doesn't mean I always choose the cheapest option. Self-hosting also costs money for hardware and maintenance, and it takes time. The least expensive option is not always the best one. I simply prefer a setup where I can understand what I'm paying for and adjust it when needed.

I don't think self-hosting is the right answer for everyone. Sometimes reducing the burden of maintenance matters more, and the reliability and convenience of an external service can be more valuable. I don't want an environment where I have to run everything myself.

I want an environment where I can make my own choices when I need to, adapt things to my goals, and decide whether I want to keep using a system. That is the main reason I'm interested in running services myself.

How I Use AI

I use AI extensively in most of my projects, especially when writing code. I describe the feature or behavior I want, AI generates code, and I review the result and decide what needs to change.

I want to be clear about this: the fact that I made a project does not mean I wrote every line of its code myself. AI often generates a substantial part of the code in my projects.

I decide what a feature should do and how I want to build it, then check whether the result matches that intent. When it doesn't, I refine the requirements or specify what to change and try again. I don't see AI generating code as incompatible with my making and running a project. I do think it is important to describe accurately what each of us contributed.

Frontend development is a particular challenge for me. I can think through what a screen should contain and how someone might use it, but turning those ideas into code is another matter. So I rely heavily on AI for frontend work. This site is also being built with AI's help as I decide its direction and make the design and implementation choices.

AI can reduce the pressure to write every line myself and lets me try ideas I might not have been able to build before. That doesn't make everything easy. Explaining a requirement clearly can be difficult, and generated results don't always match what I expected. Code existing does not prove that it works correctly. I consider checking whether it serves its intended purpose and deciding what to change part of developing with AI.

I don't always understand AI-generated code perfectly. It may use a technology or structure I'm unfamiliar with, which means I need to learn more. I don't want to hide what I don't know or pretend I built or fully understand everything myself. A more honest description of how I work is that I acknowledge what I don't know, learn when I need to, and improve the result.

Tools and Setup

Codex is the tool I use most for writing code and implementing features. I describe what I want to build, review the result, and use it to help change existing code.

I also use a local LLM that I run myself. I'm interested not only in using AI services but also in having an environment where I can run a model directly.

I have also configured my own MCP setup to connect AI with other tools and capabilities. I'm interested in how AI can go beyond generating text and work with the tools a task requires. I don't currently use a separate harness.

The point is not to use as many tools as possible, but to use the right ones when they help me get closer to the result I want. The tools and methods I use will probably change over time. What I want to keep constant is deciding what to make, checking AI's output against the real purpose, and keeping the result in a form I can manage.

To me, AI is not magic that does everything for me. It is a tool that lets me try ideas I would have struggled to implement with my current abilities alone. I don't want to hide that AI helps generate my code. I can acknowledge that and still talk honestly about what I think, the direction I choose, and what matters to me.

Why I Made This Site

This site wasn't the result of a plan I had from the beginning. It started when I impulsively bought a domain and a VPS without much of a plan. I didn't have a specific goal or even a particular need for a personal website. Only after I had the domain and server did I start thinking about what to do with them.

Buying a domain or setting up a server doesn't mean you have to build something on it. Still, once I had my own domain and server, it felt a little wasteful to leave them unused. I wondered whether to make a service with a particular feature, organize my development work, or keep it as a simple personal homepage.

But I didn't want to force a specific purpose onto it. I don't intend this site to be a job-seeking portfolio or a place to prove my abilities. Rather than trying to demonstrate how well I use technology or make my projects look impressive, I wanted a place to share the things I enjoy, the way I think, and what I might like to try.

I realized I didn't need a complete plan or all the content ready from day one. I can write about what interests me now, publish things I've written, and reshape the site when I find something new I want to make. Some pages may still be empty, and the way the site is organized now may change later. Interests change, and the site can change with them.

It also matters to me that I run this space myself. Instead of introducing myself within another service's fixed format, I can include what I need and change anything that doesn't feel right. That connects with why I like running my own systems.

I don't think running one personal website is a remarkable achievement. There are already countless personal sites on the internet, and this is just one of them. Still, I like having a small space on my own domain where I can put my thoughts in a form I choose.

I haven't decided how large this site will become or what it will contain. I don't feel it has to attract lots of visitors, and I don't want the pressure of publishing posts or adding features on a fixed schedule. I can write when I have something to say, add something I care about, and change things when I think of a better way.

What started as a question about what to do with a domain and VPS I bought on impulse has become a place where I can keep the things I enjoy in my own way. This site is less a finished product than a personal space that will keep changing and filling in over time.
