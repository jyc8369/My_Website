Bringing Back an Android Phone Stuck in Safe Mode

The volume button on an Android phone I used before broke. The hardware kept registering the button as pressed. Whenever I rebooted the phone in that state, it entered safe mode, where I could not use the apps I had installed.

Using Only Chrome for Two or Three Months

In safe mode, third-party apps are usually disabled, leaving only the apps included by the manufacturer available. Chrome still worked on my device, so for two or three months I used the phone mostly to read webtoons in Chrome.

Eventually, I started looking for another option. I found an ADB (Android Debug Bridge) command that could restrict entry into safe mode. Making the Phone Ignore the Faulty Button Input

After getting the phone to boot normally, I made a simple accessibility app to prevent the physical button input from affecting it. The app keeps the volume interface from appearing and prevents the button from triggering its usual action.

There are still limitations. I cannot easily enter modes that require button combinations, such as download mode or recovery mode. Still, I am happy that I can install and use ordinary apps again.

This Will Not Work on Every Device

This setting worked for me on an Android 15-based device. Its behavior may vary by manufacturer, model, and Android version, so the same result is not guaranteed everywhere. This method also does not repair the physical button.

Still, if a faulty volume button keeps making your phone start in safe mode, you could check whether the device supports this ADB setting. For me, it brought a phone I could barely use back to normal mode.
